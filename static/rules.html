♟ I am playing chess while you are playing checkers ⛀

PlayAbout

Each side wins by achieving the opponent's game's victory condition. This is the idea the project name is built on — you're playing chess while your opponent is playing checkers, and vice versa.

SideArmyMust achieve
Chess (White)16 standard chess piecesThe checkers win: capture every enemy piece, or leave the enemy with no legal move
Checkers (Black)24 checker menThe chess win: checkmate the enemy king

Why checkers start on both light and dark squares

A checker piece never changes the colour of square it stands on — every diagonal move or jump keeps it on the same colour for the whole game. In real checkers all the men start on one colour, which here would mean a checkers army that can only ever attack half the board. The chess king could simply stand on the other colour forever and never be checkmated. Filling ranks 6–8 with checkers on both colours is a setup change only — no checkers rule is different, every piece still steps and jumps exactly as it always has. It does mean light-square and dark-square checkers can never jump each other or chain a multi-jump between colours; they're effectively two interleaved armies that only coordinate through blocking and occupancy.

Movement

Chess pieces move by the standard FIDE rules: pawns (with double push, diagonal capture, promotion, en passant), knights, bishops, rooks, the queen, and the king (including castling). A chess piece captures a checker simply by moving onto its square — there's no special mechanic. Checkers block sliding pieces exactly like any other occupied square.

A checker man steps one square diagonally forward (toward rank 1) onto an empty square, and captures by jumping a diagonally-adjacent enemy chess piece and landing on the empty square immediately beyond it. A checker king (after a man reaches rank 1) does the same in all four diagonal directions. Checkers can never capture each other — only chess pieces are enemies to a checker.

Check and checkmate

The chess king is in check when it stands on a square a checker could jump into — that is, a checker sits diagonally adjacent to it in a direction it's allowed to capture in, and the square beyond the king is empty. As of king_capture_immunity: false (the current default, spec 7.1), the king is not protected: a checkers jump onto/through the king's square is a real, game-ending capture, exactly like capturing any other chess piece — chess gets no legality filter checkers doesn't also get. (The old behaviour — a legality filter forbidding any chess move that leaves the king attacked, producing normal pins, discovered checks, etc. — is still in the engine, gated behind RuleConfig(king_capture_immunity=True), but is no longer the default; checkers went 0-1015 under it.) Checkmate and stalemate are still scored the same way here: if the chess side has no legal move, checkers wins, whether or not the king is actually attacked. (Ordinary chess treats stalemate as a draw; here that would just be an escape hatch for a losing chess side to shed pieces and self-stalemate.)

Ending the game

  • Chess king captured → checkers wins.
  • Every checker piece captured → chess wins.
  • Checkers side to move with no legal move → chess wins.
  • Chess side to move with no legal move (checkmate or stalemate) → checkers wins.
  • Threefold repetition, or 50 moves with no capture, checker promotion, or pawn move → draw.

Checkers capturing every chess piece is not a win condition — if chess is reduced to a lone king, play continues until checkmate, stalemate, or a draw rule fires.

Configurable rules

Mandatory capture, maximal (must-take-the-longest-chain) capture, and flying kings are all standard checkers variants and can be toggled server-side; see GET /api/config for the ruleset this deployment is running.
